Write to birlikoff@gmail.com. It helps to say which iPhone and which iOS version you are on, what you expected, and what happened instead. There is no account, so nothing you send needs to identify you.

Common questions

The prayer times are wrong

Times are computed on your phone, so the two inputs are the ones to check. Open Settings → Prayer times and confirm the calculation method matches the one your local mosque uses — methods differ by several minutes, and by more for Fajr and Isha. Check the madhab as well: Hanafi puts Asr later than Shafi, Maliki and Hanbali. Then check the location; if it was skipped or read loosely you can type coordinates by hand. If the method is right but the mosque still differs by a fixed amount, use manual offsets to add or subtract minutes per prayer.

The Hijri date is a day out

The app uses the Umm al-Qura calendar, which is calculated rather than sighted, so it can differ by a day from a local moonsighting. Open Settings → Calendar → Hijri date offset and shift it by −1 or +1. Note also that the Hijri day turns at Maghrib, not midnight, as the Islamic day does — so after sunset the app already shows tomorrow's Hijri date.

Reminders are not arriving

  • Check iOS Settings → Notifications → Sunnah Habits and make sure notifications are allowed. If the permission was refused during onboarding, the app honestly shows reminders as off rather than pretending.
  • Check Focus modes, including Sleep. A Focus that filters notifications will hold a reminder back; allowing the app in that Focus fixes it.
  • In the app, Settings → Reminders must show daily reminders on. A reminder is only scheduled for a practice that is active and due that day.
  • A practice anchored to a prayer needs a location. With no location the app schedules nothing for it rather than inventing a time.
  • Reminders are built seven days ahead and rebuilt when you open the app. If the app is not opened for over a week, they run out until it is.

How do I back up or move my data

Open Settings → Export data. The app writes everything — practices, completions, streaks, exemptions and settings — to a JSON file and hands it to the share sheet, so you can save it to Files, iCloud Drive or send it to yourself. There is no sync and no cloud copy; this file is the backup. Importing it back is not available yet. Settings → Delete all data clears everything and returns the app to a fresh install.

Languages

The app is available in English, Russian, Kazakh, Turkish, Urdu and Arabic. By default it follows your phone; Settings → Language overrides that. Arabic and Urdu lay out right to left — after switching to or from one of them, close and reopen the app so the direction takes effect. Digits are Western (0–9) in every language.

Where the hadith come from, and the translations

Every practice in the app carries the hadith it rests on: the collection, the hadith number and the grading, with a link that opens the full report on sunnah.com in your browser. Nothing ships without a source.

The English text is the authored text. Hadith translations in Russian, Kazakh, Turkish, Urdu and Arabic are machine translated from the English and are marked as such in the app wherever they appear. The Arabic of the hadith is always shown, so the original is there to read. If you find a translation that misreads the Arabic, please write to us — corrections are welcome.
